## Bottom line: what to put in the application

1. **This is a cold pitch.** Neither run found a public job ad for a Japan, Asia or long-haul tour leader (2023–2026). Outbound leaders appear to come from agency pools, referrals and CVs people send in unasked. [inference] [1]–[27] [34]–[53] [54]
2. **Lead with Japan knowledge he can prove and that's useful on a trip.** Agencies sell their leaders as specialists: «Εξειδικευμένος, έμπειρος Έλληνας αρχηγός» ("specialised, experienced Greek leader") [2] and «γνώστης της ιαπωνικής κουλτούρας» ("knowledgeable about Japanese culture") [3]. Show dated Japan stays, regions, and practical know-how: rail passes and seat reservations, IC transit cards, luggage forwarding, ryokan and onsen etiquette, cherry-blossom and autumn-leaf timing, and crowd handling. [inference]
3. **Prove reliability in the agencies' own words for the job:** headcounts, timing, hotels, coaches and local guides, vouchers, expense receipts, a daily report to the office, and illness, lost passports and complaints. [35] [36] [41] [57]
4. **Cover the checks employers actually make:** excellent English, a valid first-aid certificate, availability for 10–15-day trips, and whether he can invoice or be seasonal staff. Japanese is never required, so it sets him apart rather than being a bar to entry. [34]–[45] [38] [84]
5. **Show commercial value:** clients are mostly 40–65 [30]; leaders sell optional excursions [74]; he can offer a Japan briefing evening before departure. [inference]
6. **Propose a concrete first step:** co-leader, assistant or reserve leader on a named programme, or covering back-to-back departures while based in Japan (Cosmorama says its leader sometimes waits at the destination [3]). [inference]
7. **Send Oct–Dec 2026 for spring 2027**, and again Feb–Apr 2027 for summer and autumn. [inference] [29] [40] [41]
8. **Never claim** «ξεναγός», "guide", "licensed guide", 通訳案内士, trips "led" when he was a passenger, a JLPT level he hasn't passed, or the right to work in Japan. [58] [66] [68]–[70]

## 1. Who sells Japan group trips from Greece

**The standard product [vendor claim]:** 11–13 days on the Tokyo–Hakone/Fuji–Kyoto–Nara–Osaka route (often plus Hiroshima), half board, 3–4★ hotels, one-stop flights via Istanbul, Shanghai (Juneyao), Beijing (Air China), Abu Dhabi, Dubai or Doha. Typical price €3,100–4,600 per person; premium and niche trips €4,900–7,250. Departures from Athens, sometimes Thessaloniki. [1]–[26]

**Seasons.** Cherry blossom (late March to April, plus Easter), autumn leaves (October–November), summer, and a few Christmas, New Year and Jan–Feb departures. One agent puts spring at about 60% of demand: «Τέλη Μαρτίου – μέσα Απριλίου» ("late March to mid-April"). [news] [29]

**Leader wording is near-universal** and always αρχηγός/συνοδός, never ξεναγός, for the Greek escort. Examples: «Έμπειρος ελληνόφωνος αρχηγός – συνοδός από την Αθήνα» ("experienced Greek-speaking leader-escort from Athens"); «Τοπικοί ξεναγοί - Έμπειρος αρχηγός-συνοδός από την Αθήνα» ("local guides, experienced leader-escort from Athens"). [vendor claim] [4] [6] [16]

**Resale: the seller is often not the hirer.** Run B found identical dates, prices and wording at several agencies. For example, one Air China 12-day trip is sold by Manessis, Imagination, Safe Travel and Love Your Holidays, and one autumn product by Joy Tours, Love Your Holidays and Beleon. Run A found Travel Market naming Joy Tours as organiser of a panorama product [12]. So fewer companies assign leaders than sell trips. Look for the «διοργανωτής» (organiser) field on a programme, or phone the retailer, before pitching a seller as an employer. [vendor claim + inference] [4] [5] [9] [12]–[15]

## 2. Who leads the groups and how agencies staff them

1. **Model [vendor claim].** Every Japan page that names staff uses a Greek leader-escort who flies with the group from Athens, plus local guides at sites. Specialist trips (hiking, photography) add a theme leader. [3] [4] [8] [10] [11]
2. **Destination specialists are valued [vendor claim].** Cosmorama: «σε κάποιες περιπτώσεις ο αρχηγός σας μπορεί να σας περιμένει στον προορισμό λόγω των επαναλαμβανόμενων αναχωρήσεων» ("in some cases your leader may wait for you at the destination because of our repeated departures"). Versus: «Οι αρχηγοί μας διαθέτουν βαθιά γνώση και τοπική εμπειρία» ("our leaders have deep knowledge and local experience"). So one leader may cover back-to-back departures. [1] [3]
3. **Small operators lead trips with their own team.** [23] [24]
4. **Employment model [official].** Greek training material describes tour leaders as hired by travel agencies or tour operators, either continuously or on a per-project contract. No page shows whether any named Japan operator's leader is an employee, freelancer or owner. [56]
5. **How they recruit.**
   - Agency careers pages list only office roles, but Joy Tours has a CV form and Pyramis accepts CVs at info@. [vendor claim] [5b] [88]
   - Published tour-leader hiring (non-Japan) follows a simple pattern: CV application, then an interview window, then training before the first trip. Trekking Hellas advertised on 13 Dec 2025 with interviews in Dec–Jan; Intrepid trained leaders in March 2026. [job ad] [40] [41]
   - A 2014 forum thread describes a steady flow of unsolicited CVs, says contacts matter, and advises handing CVs to Athens agencies in person. [opinion, OLD] [54]
   - Not found anywhere: trial trips, shadowing, a "Europe first" progression, or Greeks living in Japan used as leaders.
6. **An informal market [industry body, OLD 2022].** The escorts' union: «κάθε ένας μπορεί να ανέβει σε ένα τουριστικό λεωφορείο και να πει ότι είναι 'συνοδός'» ("anyone can get on a tour bus and say they are an 'escort'"). It also reports pay «μαύρα» (off the books). [55]

## 4. What employers screen for

1. **Languages [job ad].** English at "very good / excellent / fluent" level in every ad. Another language is a plus or tied to a specific guest market. **No Greek source requires Japanese or a JLPT level**, and one school's list of languages for escorts omits Japanese. [34]–[53] [84]
2. **Operational reliability [official + job ad].** The official vocational profile for «Τουριστικός Συνοδός» (tour escort) lists these duties:
   - escort groups «σε τουριστικούς προορισμούς του εξωτερικού και του εσωτερικού» ("to destinations abroad and in Greece");
   - «Διαχειρίζει έκτακτα περιστατικά, εφαρμόζοντας βασικούς κανόνες πρώτων βοηθειών» ("handles emergencies using basic first aid");
   - «Διευθετεί τα ομαδικά vouchers» ("settles group vouchers");
   - work with hotels and restaurants, and handle complaints and supplier failures;
   - «Συντάσσει καθημερινά γραπτή αναφορά (report) προς το τουριστικό γραφείο» ("writes a daily report to the agency").

   Older arbitration decisions describe «η συνοδεία, φροντίδα, διεκπεραίωση και η σωστή πραγματοποίηση ενός συγκεκριμένου προγράμματος εκδρομής» ("escorting, caring for, handling and properly carrying out a specific trip programme"), plus translating for local guides and managing flights, transfers, hotels and meals. [57] [63] [64] [35] [41]
3. **Customer-facing proof [job ad].** Either two years of customer contact [37], hospitality experience [39], or proven comparable experience [35] [42].
4. **Japan knowledge [vendor claim → inference].** Agencies market it directly [1]–[3]. Evidence that strengthens a beginner: residence or study in Japan, repeated trips, Japanese language use, route and itinerary knowledge. This is a credibility boost, **not** a published hiring threshold.
5. **Safety and temperament.** First aid is mandatory in one ad [38] and treated as essential in training material: «Βασική εκπαίδευση πρώτων βοηθειών είναι απαραίτητη» ("basic first-aid training is essential"); «Ο συνοδός πρέπει να διατηρεί ψυχραιμία, να αξιολογεί γρήγορα και να προτείνει λύσεις» ("the escort must stay calm, assess quickly and propose solutions") [84]. Every course syllabus includes first aid, complaints and travel documents. [74]–[76]
6. **Availability** for multi-week travel and peak seasons. [41]
7. **Selling optional excursions** is taught as a skill in Greek seminars, which points to agency expectations. [74]
8. **Social media / appearance.** A bonus or minor task in a few ads [40] [46]. Professional appearance appears in one ad [37]. An OLD 2019 article claims 70% of recruiters check social media. [opinion, OLD] [89]
9. **Likely rejection risks [inference].** Claiming to have *led* groups without proof; an unclear language level; describing Japan only as a tourist; no evidence of customer care or logistics; no availability for full trips; no sign of legal working status (can he invoice, is he insured); references that can't back up his claims. A recruiter notes references are cross-checked. [85] [55]

## 5. The hiring calendar

- **Trips go on sale 2–7 months ahead [vendor claim].** Fthera published a Feb 2027 trip on 9 July 2026 [26]. Signature posted spring 2026 dates on 26 Jan 2026 [16]. Imagination's early-booking discount for April–June trips ran until 28 Feb 2026 [13]. Joy's and Cosmorama's spring programmes were online in late 2025. [3] [5]
- **Tour-leader recruitment elsewhere happens 3–4 months before the season [job ad].** Trekking Hellas advertised in December for an April start; Intrepid trained leaders in March. [40] [41] Public escort ads cluster in February–April, but those are for incoming summer tourism. [48]–[52]
- **When outbound Japan leaders are assigned: not found.**
- **Recommendation [inference].** Have the CV ready now, since spring 2027 trips are already on sale. Send it **Oct–Dec 2026** for cherry-blossom season, then follow up **Feb–Apr 2027** for summer and autumn departures.

## 6. The legal and wording boundary

**Greece.**
- «Ξεναγός» (guide) is a licensed, registered profession under ν. 5121/2024 (ΦΕΚ Α' 106/15.07.2024). Article 33 defines it around interpreting museums, monuments and archaeological or cultural sites. Registration is open only to graduates of the ministry's guide schools, holders of recognised EU qualifications, or graduates of the accelerated programme. [official] [58] [59] [60]
- The older ν. 710/1977 punished unlicensed guiding with up to a year in prison and a fine up to €2,000. Whether that still applies after 2024 was not verified. [OLD] [61]
- The escort role, «Τουριστικός Συνοδός (Tour Leader)», is an official vocational (IEK/SAEK, Level 5) specialty. Law ν. 4582/2018 recognised the profession, but the escorts' union said in July 2022, and again in September 2024, that the decree setting its qualifications was still unsigned; no signing was found in 2025–2026. A 2020 draft decree proposed a licence-like certificate and sanctions on agencies using unqualified escorts; **whether it was ever enacted was not found.** Neither run found any current requirement for escorts to hold a diploma, licence or registration, and agencies don't ask for one. Recheck, because this could change. [56] [57] [55] [55b] [62]

**Japan.**
- Since 4 Jan 2018, anyone may give paid foreign-language tourist explanations: «業務独占資格から名称独占資格へと見直し…» (the qualification changed from protecting the *activity* to protecting only the *title*). Unqualified people must not use the titles 全国通訳案内士 or 地域通訳案内士 (national and regional Guide Interpreter) or anything confusingly similar. [official] [66]
- Japan's 旅程管理主任者 (tour-conductor) qualification applies to packaged tours sold by Japan-registered agencies. Whether it applies to a foreign agency's escort was not verified. [67]
- In practice Greek programmes pair a travelling escort (logistics and group care) with local guides at sites. [8] [16]

**Visa: unresolved.**
- Greece is on Japan's 90-day visa-exemption list [68]. But the Embassy in Athens words it as "for purposes without remuneration", and paid activity needs a Certificate of Eligibility [70]. Japan's foreign ministry says work for pay falls outside ordinary short stays [69].
- The permitted-activity list for short stays covers «観光…業務連絡その他これらに類似する活動» ("tourism… business liaison and similar activities") and doesn't mention tour conductors [71]. A law-firm note says pay from a foreign employer is "not always" acceptable [73].
- **The agency should confirm with the Embassy before assigning him a trip.** Japan plans an online travel pre-authorisation (JESTA) for FY2028 [72].

**Safe CV titles:** «Αρχηγός-Συνοδός Εκδρομών» (matches agency wording), «Τουριστικός Συνοδός» (the official specialty), "Tour Leader", "Tour Manager", "Tour Escort", "Group Leader". For destination knowledge, write "cultural briefings / destination commentary for the group".
**Avoid:** «ξεναγός», «ξενάγηση», "guide" or "tour guide" on its own, "licensed guide", 通訳案内士.

## 7. Pay and working terms

- **Japan escort pay: not found.** Trip prices are what the customer pays and say nothing about the leader's fee.
- **Old official scales for employed escorts [official, OLD]:**
  - Arbitration decision 10/2008: €81.50 per 8-hour day for groups up to 50. The employer pays all travel, a single room, and meals abroad or €26.60 per meal. Cancellation with under 48 hours' notice is paid in full, and medical costs abroad are covered. [63]
  - Arbitration decision 15/2010: €83.50 per day and €28 per meal abroad. [64]
  - Whether these still apply after the 2012 labour reforms is not established. The 2025 collective agreement for travel-agency staff (+4%, then +5% from May 2026) covers office staff, not escorts. [65]
- **Recent local ads, not Japan:** €1,000–1,200 net/month (Athens day trips), €1,300/month (Kos), €1,800 (Crete, period not stated). [43] [47] [50]
- **Anecdotal [opinion, OLD 2014]:** €40–200/day, "most closer to €40"; always paid per working day; «το να τρως φέσι και να πληρώνεσαι 5-6 μήνες μετά το ταξίδι δεν είναι καθόλου ανήκουστο» ("getting stiffed or paid 5–6 months late is not unheard of"). [54]
- **Contract models:** permanent or per-project [56]; fixed-term or freelance invoice [38]; freelance with travel, accommodation and some meals covered (Intrepid, a Greece/Europe role, not Japan) [41]. The union reports undeclared pay [55].
- **Tips.** Japan programmes quote about €80 per person for the driver and local services [14]. Whether the Greek leader gets tips: not found.
- **Ask each operator for these in writing:** fee and paid preparation days; flights, hotel, meals, insurance; expense float and receipts; cancellation rules; employment vs invoicing; payment timing; whether tips or excursion commissions count as pay.

## 8. Entry path, training and competition

**Credentials (none required; all help):**
- **First aid:** Hellenic Red Cross, 6-hour CPR/AED course €20 or 12-hour course €50, valid 3 years. The cheapest credible thing to add, and it answers a real ad requirement. [77] [38]
- **Tour-leader training:**
  - ΕΚΠΑ (University of Athens) e-learning "Τουριστικός Συνοδός": 115 h, €500 in instalments or €475 paid at once (€425 early-booking price until 9 Oct; confirm on the page), starts 19 Oct 2026, **apply by 9 Oct 2026** [75]
  - EUROTraining e-learning: 150 h, starts 30 Sep, 15 Oct, 2 Nov and 1 Dec 2026 [76]
  - ALFA Studies seminar: covers optional-excursion selling and first aid, with a network of «4.000 και πλέον» (4,000+) partner businesses [74]
  - Full IEK diploma: 2–4 semesters [76]

  None promises placement, and no agency names a preferred course. [vendor claim]
- **JLPT (the official Japanese exam):** held in Athens in December only, through the Greek-Japanese Association. The 2024 registration window was 1–15 Sept at €70, so the 2026 window has most likely closed; confirm. [78] [79]
- **JNTO "Japan Travel Specialist":** not available for Greece; the US version was suspended in 2024. JNTO's Rome office covers Greece and could be asked. [80] [81] [82]

**Networking and visibility:** Greek-Japanese Association (lessons, JLPT, events) [79]; Japan was honoured country at the Thessaloniki International Fair, Sept 2026 [83]; Japan Festival Athens (April); AnimeCon Thessaloniki (3–4 Oct 2026). These are places to give Japan talks or meet niche operators. [inference]

**Competition [inference].** About 20 agencies need Greek Japan leaders every season, and demand is growing. Many agencies advertise "experienced" leaders, but no one has built a public "Japan specialist leader" brand, apart from small operators leading their own trips. Nobody has counted Japan escorts or applicants. The newcomer stands out through **provable Japan depth plus professional reliability**, not a record of trips led that he can't have yet. [1]–[26]

**Realistic first steps [inference]:** target own-product and niche operators; offer to co-lead, assist or be a reserve leader; accept shorter European trips to build a record; give Japan talks at agencies' pre-departure evenings.

## 9. The application

**Format.** 1–2 pages; «Το βιογραφικό και η συνοδευτική επιστολή είναι πάντα στη γλώσσα της αγγελίας» ("the CV and cover letter are always in the language of the ad") [85]. For unsolicited CVs to Greek agencies, write it in Greek and keep an English version ready. A professional headshot suits public-facing roles (no holiday photo or selfie) [86] [87]; one ad explicitly asks for a photo [45]. No evidence that agencies prefer Europass. Deliver by email, web form (Joy Tours), or in person in Athens [54].

**Headline: the runs disagree.**
- Run A: if he has never led a tour, use "Υποψήφιος τουριστικός συνοδός με γνώση της Ιαπωνίας" (tour-leader candidate with knowledge of Japan).
- Run B: «Αρχηγός-Συνοδός Εκδρομών – Ιαπωνία» (tour leader-escort – Japan).
- **Recommendation:** make the headline the target role, not a claim of history: «Αρχηγός-Συνοδός Εκδρομών | Εξειδίκευση Ιαπωνία» / "Tour Leader – Japan Specialist". "Υποψήφιος" tells the reader "beginner" before anything else. Honesty belongs in the experience section: only trips he actually led are listed as "led". [inference]

**Sections:**
1. **Profile:** 3 lines covering Japan depth, reliability and availability, echoing the agencies' «εξειδικευμένος… με τοπική εμπειρία» ("specialised… with local experience").
2. **Japan experience:** a table of trips (dates, length, regions, purpose; residence or study) plus practical know-how (rail, IC cards, luggage forwarding, ryokan/onsen, seasonal timing, crowd handling at hotspots). Keep **visited**, **researched** and **professionally led** clearly separate.
3. **Group and customer-facing experience:** numbers (group size, days, budget) and the duty words from section 4.
4. **Operational skills:** incident handling, suppliers and vouchers, cash and receipts, daily reporting, coordinating local guides.
5. **Languages:** Greek; English (CEFR level or certificate); Japanese (JLPT, or an honest self-assessment).
6. **Training and certificates:** first aid (issuer, expiry date); course, if taken.
7. **Availability and work status:** 10–15-day trips, seasons, short notice, can stay in Japan between departures; can invoice (μπλοκάκι) or be seasonal staff.
8. **References:** people who have agreed to vouch for his reliability and group handling.
9. *Optional:* a link to a professional Japan Instagram or blog.

**Cover note (per agency, short):** name one of their programmes; offer a concrete first role (co-leader, reserve, back-to-back departures); offer a free Japan briefing for their customers; state availability for the coming season. Variety Cruises-style employers also expect a motivation letter. [42]

**Never claim:** «ξεναγός», "guide" or licensed status in Greece or Japan; 通訳案内士; trips "led" that he joined as a traveller; fluent Japanese or a JLPT level without proof; a first-aid certificate he doesn't hold; the right to work in Japan; any link to JNTO or the Embassy.

## 12. Gaps and caveats

- No Japan-specific or long-haul escort ad, selection process, trial-trip practice or pay scale was found. Most ad evidence is for tours of Greece or Europe.
- Some job sites couldn't be read. Run A could read LinkedIn, but xe.gr was blocked. Run B was blocked from xe.gr and LinkedIn, rate-limited on Indeed, and hit a CAPTCHA on careerjet. Some ads were seen only as titles, e.g. "Ζητείται Συνοδός Εκδρομών (Μισθός 1.400€)" (escort wanted, €1,400 salary).
- The legal points weren't checked against the enacted text: ν. 5121/2024 art. 33/40 details; whether ν. 710/1977 still applies; whether the escort decree under ν. 4582/2018 was ever signed; whether 旅程管理主任者 applies to foreign escorts.
- Whether a paid Greek escort can work in Japan on visa-free entry is **unresolved** by official sources.
- Pay data is OLD (2008/2010) or anecdotal (2014).
- There is no official count of Greek visitors to Japan; the growth figures are agents' estimates.
- The companies behind the shared (resold) products weren't identified. Some pages omit group size, organiser or full departure counts, and "from" prices may exclude taxes.
- Run B read pages through a fetch tool that summarised them. Spot-check Greek quotes before public use.
