# Client intake for premium CV writing — research

Checked: 2026-09-25. Recheck after: 2027-03-25.
Question: how do top CV writers, coaches and recruiters run client intake, and what should CVgen's intake ask?
Review: research-reviewer round 1 FINDINGS, round 2 PASS with its notes applied (`docs/work/client-workflow/reviews/research-01.md`, `research-02.md`).

Labels used: **[vendor claim]** = a paid service describing its own process; **[opinion]** = practitioner judgement, not data; **[survey]** = data from a survey or study, with its sample; **[unverified]** = could not be confirmed at a primary source.

## Summary

- **Many services use a form first, then a conversation.** NRWA says "many writers use some combination" of questionnaire and phone consultation, while some work only by email and some only by phone [S11]; Career Professionals of Canada recommends a questionnaire before the consultation [S1]. Both are older: 2017 and 2018.
- **Budget and premium services differ mostly in how deep the intake goes.**
  - Volume services use a form of "a few minutes" (TopResume) or "90 seconds, tops!" (ResumeSpice). A call is a paid add-on at TopResume and part of the package at ResumeSpice [S2][S3][S4].
  - Premium examples: one executive firm asks for 1–2 hours of form-filling plus a 60-minute call [S7]. One writer uses a 90-minute call and no form [S9]. Another sizes the call to career length: 30 minutes for 0–2 years, up to 90 minutes for 15+ years [S6].
- **Intake usually happens after payment, sometimes with a fit call before it.**
  - TopResume and ResumeSpice send the questionnaire right after purchase [S2][S4].
  - A professional association reports that "many practitioners require payment IN FULL" before starting [S1].
  - Find My Profession takes a short questionnaire, then a call, and discusses payment on that call [S5, not opened].
  - Briefcase Coach requires "a no-obligation discovery call to book our services" [S8].
- **At least two premium writers skip the form entirely** [S6][S9]. A third is reported to [S10, unverified].
  - Resume Target's reason: "A form is only as good as the questions you ask yourself—if you knew what to include on your resume, you'd write it yourself." A live interviewer can ask follow-up questions [S6]. **[vendor claim]**
- **The core questions are the same everywhere.** They cover:
  - the target (title, level, industry, type of organisation);
  - real job ads;
  - per role: the scope and 3–5 accomplishments, in Challenge-Action-Result form;
  - strengths and "brand";
  - extras such as education, training, languages and awards [S2][S15][S16][S17].
- **Clients leave out outcomes and numbers.** Asked "how much?", they answer "I don't know", "I don't remember" or "a lot" [S20]. Clients "don't speak in bullet points" [S12]. Writers probe on:
  - money saved or earned, and time saved [S18];
  - who else was eligible for an award [S19];
  - what others and appraisals say about the client [S15];
  - "Accomplished X as measured by Y by doing Z" [S21].
- **The target role and evidence of results matter most; the numbers come from vendor-run data.**
  - Jobscan recruiter survey (384 recruiters, Feb–Mar 2025): 76.4% filter by skills, 55.3% by job title, and 58% value quantifiable results [S23].
  - Separately, Jobscan's own tool data (over 2.5 million applications, period not stated, correlational): applicants whose title matched the posting got **10.6x** more interviews [S23].
  - Resume Genius, 2026 (1,500 US hiring managers, 4–6 June 2026): 42% are put off by "vague or generic descriptions of previous job responsibilities" [S33].
  - Resume Genius, 2024 (625 hiring managers): 44% called "no measurable achievements" a red flag [S24].
- **Readers skim fast** — an average of 7.4 seconds in Ladders' 2018 eye-tracking study [S22]. Titles, dates and scope therefore have to be exact. The reason is the title filtering above [S23] and the fact that practitioner forms collect scope explicitly [S15][S16]. The often-quoted Ladders gaze order could not be verified.
- **Things that raise red flags must be surfaced at intake** so the writer can handle them. Resume Genius 2026: 65% flag job-hopping, 43% a long or unexplained gap, 49% heavily AI-generated materials [S33].
- **Keeping clients to the end of a form:**
  - Keep the form short and add a call, or replace it with a call [S4][S6].
  - Say upfront how long it takes and what to have ready.
  - One benchmark dataset (Zuko; sample size not given) shows 45% of form viewers and 66% of form starters finish, and says the number of fields "is not necessarily correlated with completion rate" [S26].
- **Intake must ask the target country and channel, because the rules on personal details differ:**
  - The UK advises leaving out date of birth, age, marital status and nationality, and a photo [S27][S28].
  - The old Europass template guide (2002–2010) marks date of birth, nationality, gender and photo as optional [S29]. The current Europass site's tips say "add a professional photograph of yourself" [S34].
  - One Romanian crewing agency requires date of birth, nationality and passport details [S30].
  - Length norm: "no longer than two A4 pages" in the UK [S28].

## 1. Format, timing, length, premium vs budget

1. Career Professionals of Canada (an association, **2017**) recommends a questionnaire that the client completes "at the client's convenience prior to a scheduled consultation session". The consultation follows by phone, in person or by email, with a time limit set in advance [S1].
2. The same source: "Many practitioners require payment IN FULL … prior to starting the project" [S1].
3. The US National Résumé Writers' Association (NRWA), **2018**: "Many writers use some combination of questionnaires and phone consultations. Some work strictly by email; some work only over the phone." A reputable writer will refer on a client who prefers a different style [S11].
4. **TopResume [vendor claim]**
   - "Upon placing your order, you'll be directed to a questionnaire asking for information about your career."
   - It asks for the current résumé, job listings of interest, key strengths and major accomplishments, and job-search concerns [S2].
   - The FAQ says the questionnaire "should only take a few minutes to complete" [S3].
   - A phone consultation with the writer is a paid add-on [S2].
5. **ResumeSpice [vendor claim]**
   - After purchase the client gets a link to a questionnaire ("90 seconds, tops!") and a link to book a consultation.
   - The consultation is an "in-depth phone conversation" to "expand on the online questionnaire" [S4].
6. **Find My Profession [vendor claim, not opened]:** a short questionnaire, then a call to discuss goals and fit, then the payment options are explained. So the questionnaire and the call both come before payment [S5]. *(The page did not render when fetched; this rests on the search-result summary of the page.)*
7. **Career Keystone, executive [vendor claim]** — one firm's example:
   - Questionnaire within 2 business days of purchase. "Most clients spend 1–2 hours."
   - It covers "your career history, target direction, key achievements, and leadership story".
   - Then a 60-minute strategy call to "go deeper into your career narrative, leadership style, and goals" [S7].
8. **Briefcase Coach, executive, $1,500–$3,900 [vendor claim]**
   - "We do require a no-obligation discovery call to book our services. This call gives you the opportunity to ask questions, ensure a mutual fit, and align on expectations."
   - The page gives no call length: "90 minutes" appears only in a client testimonial. The page does not mention a questionnaire either way [S8].
9. **Resume Target [vendor claim]**
   - No questionnaire. Interview length depends on experience: 30 min (0–2 years), 45 (3–5), 60 (6–14), 90 (15+).
   - The client must share "1-2 job postings you're targeting" before the call [S6].
10. **Final Draft Resumes, executive [practitioner]:** "I don't use questionnaires". Instead there is a "90-minute conversation about your career, your leadership impact, and what you're targeting next" [S9].
11. **Great Resumes Fast [vendor claim, unverified]:** reported to use no intake form. It holds a 60–90 minute call, and before it the client sends the latest résumé, a LinkedIn link and any target jobs [S10].
12. PARWCC, the US résumé writers' association, **2026** [opinion]: "Client intake is a big time sink. You will spend a lot of time on intake forms and discovery calls before you ever write a word." The real work is "probing vague statements, reconstructing timelines, and translating experience into business outcomes" [S12].
13. PARWCC, **2025**, recommends "intake forms and onboarding emails to set expectations" and says to "Ask smart intake questions that uncover impact, not just duties" [S14].

## 2. The core question set (what they always ask)

14. **Target.** The Enelow–Kursmark executive worksheet (by the founders of the Resume Writing Academy, 2014) opens with:
    - "Target Job Title(s)";
    - "Job Scope";
    - "Type of Organization";
    - "Preferred Industries";
    - "Compensation Expectation" [S15].
15. **Target, framed as three parts.** Brooklyn Resume Studio asks "What are you targeting?" and splits it into desired functions, career level and work environment [S17].
16. **Real job ads.** They are requested by:
    - TopResume ("job listings of interest") [S2];
    - Resume Target (1–2 postings, compulsory) [S6];
    - Wise Resume Writers ("Important: Please provide 3 links to positions you wish to apply for in order of preference"; the form enforces at least one link) [S16].
17. **Per role.**
    - Enelow–Kursmark ask for:
      - company, location, tenure, title and dates;
      - "Scope (P&L responsibility, number of direct and indirect reports, departments managed, budget size)";
      - "Specific business challenges you faced";
      - what you did, "written In CAR story format – Challenge / Action / Result – to include long-term strategic impact" [S15].
    - Wise asks for:
      - the actual title and the working title;
      - the "Title of person you report to";
      - the "Number of people you supervise" [S16].
18. **Per role, 3–5 accomplishments.** Wise asks, for each role: "Briefly describe 3 to 5 of your accomplishments in this position… Use numbers wherever possible. Give facts and figures." [S16].
19. **Brand and self-image.** Enelow–Kursmark prompts:
    - "How would others describe me?";
    - "How would I describe myself?";
    - "What makes you different from your peers?";
    - "When and why have I been successful where others have failed?" [S15].
    - Brooklyn Resume Studio adds "How do you want to position yourself?" and "What do you feel is the core value you've brought to your role?" [S17].
20. **Differentiators.** Wise: "What are the key strengths that you want to highlight… What makes you stand out from your competitors?" [S16].
21. **Extras.**
    - Enelow–Kursmark list business and community leadership, publications, "Language fluency, international experience", technical proficiency, and family or personal activities [S15].
    - Wise has sections for:
      - education;
      - professional development;
      - military service;
      - memberships;
      - publications;
      - patents;
      - computer skills;
      - languages;
      - global experience;
      - awards;
      - volunteer work [S16].
22. **Existing documents.** The old résumé is TopResume's "primary source of information" [S3]. Clients without one get "a brief questionnaire that will request all the information we need" [S3].
23. **What is wrong now.** Brooklyn Resume Studio asks "What challenges are you having with the current resume?" [S17].

## 3. What clients forget, and how writers probe

24. **Outcomes are the missing piece.** Robin Ryan, a career coach, writing in Forbes (2024-12-03): typical answers when asked how much a client saved are "I don't know," "I don't remember," or "a lot". She follows up: "If you say you saved money, I ask how much?" [S20]. **[opinion]**
25. **Clients describe duties, not impact.** "Clients don't speak in bullet points". Writers get raw lines such as "I answered emails" and have to extract the impact [S12].
26. **Money and time prompts.** Virginia Franco (NCRW, CPRW) asks five questions:
    1. What makes me proud?
    2. Did I save money?
    3. Did I save time?
    4. Are people happier because of me? (for example retention)
    5. Did I improve the company's circumstances, even as part of a team? [S18] **[opinion, 2015]**
27. **Prompts for results without numbers.** Kamara Toffolo (via Jobscan) asks:
    - "What have I led my team to achieve?";
    - "How did I repair an important relationship…?";
    - "How did I automate a process?";
    - when promotions were earned and why (source wording: "When did I earn them and why did I earn them?");
    - why an award was received and who else was eligible for it (paraphrase) (the competition gives the award its context) [S19].
28. **The "what would others say" probe.** Enelow–Kursmark: "What have others said about you? What characteristics have repeatedly cropped up in performance evaluations? What are you 'known for'…?" [S15]. This is the published form of the "what would your boss say" technique.
29. **Structured bullet formulas used as probes.** Challenge-Action-Result (CAR) [S13][S15]. Laszlo Bock (former Google people chief): "Accomplished [X] as measured by [Y] by doing [Z]" [S21].
30. **Scope gets under-reported, so practitioner forms ask for it explicitly.**
    - Enelow–Kursmark: P&L, direct and indirect reports, departments and budget [S15].
    - Wise: reporting line and number supervised [S16].
    - Recruiters filter on job title (55.3%) and years of experience (over 44%) [S23]. So exact titles, dates and scope are what the intake must capture.
31. **Forms can't follow up; a live interviewer can.**
    - Resume Target justifies its calls this way: a trained interviewer asks follow-up questions that uncover forgotten achievements [S6]. **[vendor claim]**
    - Final Draft Resumes says its call is there to "find the story your resume needs to tell" [S9].

## 4. Which questions matter most, and what hiring readers look for

32. **Ladders eye-tracking study, 2018** (reported by HR Dive on 2018-11-08) [S22] **[survey]**:
    - average first skim: 7.4 seconds, up from 6 seconds in 2012;
    - layouts that did well: simple, with clear sections and headings;
    - layouts that did badly: "cluttered layouts, a lack of white space on the page, multiple columns and long sentences".
    - **[unverified]** The often-cited sample (30 recruiters over 10 weeks) and gaze order (title and company, then dates, then education) are not supported by HR Dive or the Ladders press coverage checked in review, and the primary PDF returned 403. Do not rely on them.
33. **Jobscan "State of the Job Search 2025"** [S23] — two separate data sets:
    - **(a) Recruiter survey** [survey, vendor-run] — 384 recruiters, February–March 2025:
      - filter by skills: 76.4%;
      - by education: 59.7%;
      - by job title: 55.3%;
      - by certifications and licences: 50.6%;
      - by years of experience: over 44%;
      - by location: 43.4%.
      - Also: 58.18% value quantifiable results; 55.32% value tailored applications.
    - **(b) Tool data** [vendor, correlational] — "over 2.5 million job applications" from job seekers using Jobscan's tools, no period given:
      - applicants whose title matched the target posting showed 10.6x higher interview rates;
      - this is a correlation in a self-selected user base, not a controlled result.
34. **Resume Genius hiring surveys** [survey, vendor-run, Pollfish]:
    - **2026** (1,500 US hiring managers, 4–6 June 2026) [S33]. Red flags:
      - job-hopping: 65% ("the leading red flag");
      - heavily AI-generated application materials: 49%;
      - "a long or unexplained employment gap": 43%;
      - "vague or generic descriptions of previous job responsibilities": 42%.
      - The 2026 report has no figures on length or measurable achievements.
    - **2024** (625 US hiring managers) [S24] — kept only for figures the 2026 report lacks:
      - 44% called "no measurable achievements" a red flag;
      - 54% expected two-page résumés;
      - work experience was ranked the most important section.
35. **NACE Job Outlook 2025** (237 employers; students and entry level) [S25] **[survey]**: nearly 90% look for evidence of problem-solving, almost 80% for teamwork, and at least 70% for written communication, initiative and work ethic. This is less relevant to experienced hires.
36. **What practitioners say matters most [opinion]:**
    - TopResume: "Your resume only tells us what you did in the past, and we need some insight into your future goals so we can help you get there" [S2].
    - PARWCC: clients hire a writer to answer "How do I position myself so someone wants to hire me?" [S14].
    - The judgement work (what to leave out, tone, ATS) is the hard part: "Writing Is the easy part" [S12].
37. **Synthesis.** The sources point to three highest-leverage question areas:
    1. target title and level, plus real job ads (title and skills filtering, tailoring; claims 33a/b);
    2. evidenced outcomes per role, quantified where possible (claims 24, 33a, 34: vague descriptions 42% in 2026; no measurable achievements 44% in 2024);
    3. exact titles, dates and scope, plus anything that looks like job-hopping or an unexplained gap, so the writer can frame it (claims 30, 34).
    - No study directly ranks intake questions by their effect on the final CV.

## 5. Completion: keeping clients to the end

38. The main tactic is short forms paired with a call: 90 seconds plus a call at ResumeSpice [S4], and "a few minutes" at TopResume [S3]. At the premium end, one firm accepts a long form (1–2 hours) because a call follows [S7], and at least two writers drop the form for a call [S6][S9].
39. The practitioner case against forms is quality, not only drop-off: a form cannot ask follow-up questions [S6]. **[vendor claim]**
40. **Web form data** [S26] **[benchmark, general web forms, not CV intake]**. Source: Zuko's customer benchmarks, checked at source by CueFully; sample size not given.
    - 45% view-to-completion; 66% of those who start finish.
    - Field count "is not necessarily correlated with completion rate": local-government forms average 71 inputs and still reach 85% completion.
    - Mobile completes lower than desktop: 42% vs 47%.
    - The popular "multi-step forms convert 86% better" claim could not be traced to any source.
    - Reading: motivation and clarity matter more than raw length. A paying client with a stake behaves more like the government-form user than a cold lead. **[my inference]**
41. **Optional by default.** Wise's form enforces only four inputs: name, mobile, email and one target-job link. The rest is asked, not enforced: "Not all questions may apply to you. If they do not apply, mark them N/A." [S16]. Enelow–Kursmark present the worksheets as a "permanent record" the client can reuse for interviews and applications, which gives the client a reason to do the work [S15].
42. **No CV-specific drop-off data was found** (see Gaps).

## 6. Country and market differences that intake must ask about

43. **UK, National Careers Service (government):** do not include "your age, your date of birth, whether you're married or your nationality" [S27].
44. **UK, Prospects:** no photo unless acting or modelling; "Your date of birth is irrelevant"; "We recommend CVs are no longer than two A4 pages", with exceptions for academics and long careers [S28].
45. **Europass** — the EU's official CV format:
    - **The old template guide** (Cedefop, © 2002–2010) marks "Nationality", "Date of birth" and "Gender" each "(optional)"; "Insert photograph if requested (optional)"; "in most cases, two pages are enough" [S29]. This is the 2002–2010 guide, not current guidance.
    - **The current Europass CV page** does not say which fields are optional. Its polishing tips say "add a professional photograph of yourself" [S34].
    - So there is no single EU norm; ask the client and check the ad.
46. **One crewing agency (Cuza Crewing, Romania, 2025):**
    - asks for "full name, contact details, date of birth, nationality, and passport details";
    - sea service with "vessel types, tonnage, and duration";
    - all valid certificates: STCW, endorsements and medicals [S30] **[vendor]**.
    - This is one agency, not an industry standard. It suggests marine CVs can sit at the opposite end from UK norms, so intake must know whether the CV goes to a crewing agency, a shipowner or a shore employer.
47. **US student guidance (Georgetown):** "You do not need to list your immigration status on your resume… You should never lie about your immigration status" [S31]. So intake should ask about work authorisation to decide *whether* to show it, not assume it goes on the page.
48. **Germany:** vendor blogs say a photo, date of birth and nationality are usual, but legally optional under the AGG (Germany's anti-discrimination law). No official source could be opened (see Gaps). Treat as "ask the client / check the ad".

## Draft question set (synthesis)

This is **my synthesis**, not a published instrument. It assumes a short written form plus a follow-up probe, done either by a call or by the AI asking follow-up questions on the answers; the follow-up exists because forms can't dig [S6][S12]. Claim numbers in brackets.

### Core (15)

| # | Question | Must / optional | Why (claims) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | What role or rank are you applying for next, and at what level? | Must | Title filtering; title match correlates with interviews; the future goal drives the CV (14, 15, 33, 36) |
| 2 | Which country or countries, and which kind of employer (crewing agency, shipowner, shore-based company, other)? | Must | Decides photo, date of birth, nationality and length (43–48) |
| 3 | Paste or link 1–3 real job ads you would apply to (or name target companies). | Must | Asked by TopResume, Resume Target and Wise; supports tailoring (16, 33a) |
| 4 | Upload your current CV, LinkedIn link and any old CVs. | Must | Primary source of facts (4, 11, 22) |
| 5 | Upload what proves your record: certificates, sea-service or discharge book, appraisals, references, awards. | Must (upload), content optional | Certifications are filtered on; appraisals feed the "others say" probe (28, 33a, 46) |
| 6 | For your last 2–3 roles: exact title, dates, employer, and scope (team size, who you report to, budget, vessel type and size, fleet, area). | Must | Practitioner forms ask for scope explicitly; recruiters filter by title and years (17, 30, 33a) |
| 7 | For each of those roles: the 2–3 things you are proudest of — what the problem was, what you did, what changed. | Must | Asked in every intake reviewed; CAR framing; vague duty descriptions are a red flag (17, 18, 26, 29, 34) |
| 8 | Did any of that save money or time, cut incidents or downtime, pass an audit, or grow something? Rough numbers are fine. | Must | Practitioners say outcomes are most often missing; quantified results are valued by recruiters (24, 26, 33a, 34) |
| 9 | What would your last manager or crew say you are known for? What keeps coming up in your appraisals? | Must | Brand probe with evidence behind it (19, 28) |
| 10 | What makes you different from others at your level? | Must | Asked in practitioner forms (19, 20) |
| 11 | Anything we must handle carefully: gaps, short contracts, a career change, something to leave out? | Must | Deciding what to omit is the hard judgement; 2026: job-hopping 65%, unexplained gap 43% red flags (12, 34) |
| 12 | Where are you willing to work, when are you available, and do you need a visa or sponsorship? | Must | Location is filtered on; decides whether the status appears on the CV (33a, 47) |
| 13 | Which personal details are you comfortable showing: photo, date of birth, nationality? | Must | Market-dependent; the client's consent (43–46) |
| 14 | What isn't working with your current CV (no replies, wrong roles, too long)? | Optional | Sets concrete goals (23) |
| 15 | Any awards, promotions or firsts, and who else was in the running? | Optional | Evidence without numbers, with context (27) |

### Optional block (skippable, reused later)

- Education and training beyond what the certificates show; languages and their level; software and systems.
- Memberships, publications, volunteer work, military service (21).
- Compensation expectation, used only for positioning, never printed (14).
- Tone and positioning: "How do you want to come across?" (19).
- Deadline and how the CV will be sent (agency portal, email, applicant tracking system (ATS, the screening software employers use)) — affects layout choices (33a).

### Format recommendation (synthesis)

- **Before payment:** at most a 2-minute fit check (questions 1–2). Find My Profession (unverified) and Briefcase Coach qualify the client before the sale (6, 8).
- **After payment:** questions 3–13 as a form that takes about 10–15 minutes, uploads first. State the time upfront. Enforce only the essentials; make everything else skippable (38, 40, 41).
- **Then the probe:** a live or AI follow-up on questions 7–9. This is where premium services earn their price (7–11, 31).

## Gaps and caveats

- **No CV-specific completion or drop-off data.** Nothing public shows how many résumé clients abandon intake forms. The only numbers are generic web-form benchmarks, with no stated sample size [S26]. A search-engine summary suggested figures such as "15% of executive clients never complete questionnaires" and "reminders boost completion 40–50%", but I could not trace them to a source, so they are excluded.
- **Associations don't publish their standard intake instruments.** PARWCC, CDI and NRWA do not publish them openly; their certification material is behind paywalls. The best primary question set found is the Enelow–Kursmark worksheet [S15], from 2014. The association process descriptions I did find are 2017–2018 [S1][S11]; the PARWCC pieces are current but are opinion [S12][S13][S14].
- **Most process descriptions are vendor marketing** [S2–S10], with timings as each vendor states them. Two pages could not be opened: Great Resumes Fast (403) and Find My Profession (empty render). Their claims rest on search summaries.
- **The hiring-reader data comes mostly from vendors** that sell résumé tools:
  - Jobscan survey: n=384, Feb–Mar 2025.
  - Jobscan tool data: 2.5M applications, correlational, no period given.
  - Resume Genius: n=1,500 in June 2026, and n=625 in 2024.
  - All are US-centric and self-reported.
  - Ladders 2018: only the 7.4-second figure is supported by secondary coverage. The sample size and gaze order are unverified and the primary PDF was blocked.
- **No hard evidence ranks which intake question most improves the final CV.** Section 4's ranking is inferred from what readers filter on and flag, plus practitioner opinion.
- **Country norms are thin.**
  - Germany rests only on vendor blogs; the official Make it in Germany page was blocked by a bot check.
  - The EU position is split between an old Europass guide (fields optional) and the current Europass tips (add a photo).
  - Country norms outside the UK were not verified at an official current source.
- **Marine-specific intake practice** (how maritime CV writers or crewing managers run intake) was covered by only one Romanian crewing agency [S30]. Worth a dedicated follow-up if marine is the first market.
- **Visas, relocation and gaps** as intake topics are not described in any practitioner intake form I could open. The draft question set includes them based on the recruiter filters [S23], the Resume Genius 2026 red flags [S33] and the Georgetown guidance [S31], not on a published intake instrument.
