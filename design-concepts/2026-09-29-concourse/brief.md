# Concourse

**Idea in one line:** the CV is the signage of a Japanese station. The name sits on a station-name board (katakana, Greek and Latin stacked over a line band with the neighbouring stops), the Japan record on a colour-coded LED departure indicator, the know-how in a pictogram set, and the pitch on a yellow meeting-point sign, with tactile paving underfoot.
Three words: *Japanese station wayfinding*.

Idea run 2026-09-29 (magazine-editor). `Domain` Travel & Tourism; specialty Japan; role Αρχηγός-Συνοδός Εκδρομών. Status: proposed.

## What the owner should notice first

The station board. マルコス・イリアディス over «Μάρκος Ηλιάδης» over "Markos Iliadis", as a real board stacks its three scripts. An orange line band carries the role, and below it the neighbours sit left and right: ◀ Lazuli Travel (where he is now) and "επόμενη στάση: η ομάδα σας ▶" (next stop: your group). Then the black LED departure board listing his five Japan trips in amber, with coloured train-type labels (green ΤΑΞΙΔΙ, amber ΣΠΟΥΔΕΣ, red ΣΥΝ-ΑΡΧΗΓΙΑ) and the kanji of every place.

## `Three-Second Test`

- **Travel:** station signage, the departure board ("ΑΝΑΧΩΡΗΣΕΙΣ", 発車案内), the plane, train and luggage pictograms, the direction arrow.
- **Japan:** the three-script station board with katakana, kanji place names on the LED board, 日本 on the hanging sign, yellow tactile paving (invented in Japan), onsen and sakura pictograms.
- **Escort:** a pictogram of a leader raising a flag with the group behind, the yellow "meeting point" sign (the escort *is* the meeting point), and "η ομάδα σας" as the next stop.

## References and the principle taken from each

1. Japanese station-name boards, ekimeihyō (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Running_in_board). **Principle:** the current station's name is largest and centred, the scripts are stacked, a line-colour band runs across, and the previous and next stops sit left and right under arrows. Taken as the identity block. No railway company's colours, typeface or line codes are copied: the band is our own orange and the "JP 05" badge is invented.
2. Tokyo 1964 pictogram system (Eye: https://eyemagazine.com/blog/post/year-of-the-pictograms; Japan House London: https://www.japanhouselondon.uk/read-and-watch/designing-for-the-world-japanese-pictograms-at-the-tokyo-1964-olympics/). **Principle:** a coordinated set of silhouettes that speaks to visitors who cannot read Japanese. All twelve pictograms here are drawn anew.
3. Tactile paving, tenji blocks (https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tactile_paving; Japan Up Close: https://japanupclose.web-japan.org/tech/t20211008_1.html). **Principle:** the floor as information, with dots for "attention" and bars for "go this way". Used as the page's edge.
4. Japanese LED departure indicators (Wikimedia Commons category: https://commons.wikimedia.org/wiki/Category:LED_displays_at_train_stations_in_Japan). **Principle:** a black panel, amber text, and colour-coded train-type labels that tell you the kind of service before its name. Here the kind of trip is coded (travel, study, co-led group). The LED look is a dot-matrix mask over normal type, so the text stays real and selectable.
5. Fira Sans (Google Fonts specimen: https://fonts.google.com/specimen/Fira+Sans). **Principle:** a humanist grotesque with a true Black weight and full Greek, in the tradition of public-information type.

## How it differs

- **From Flagship:** Flagship is a portrait hero with ornament. This is a wayfinding system; the typography and the colour codes carry the page.
- **From the other two styles:** a hard, architectural, sans-serif voice (Fira Black, charcoal, signal yellow, orange, LED amber) against Woodblock's painted serif print and Stamp Rally's paper ephemera.
- **From the rejected "Line Diagram":** no route line, no dots on a rail, no numbered stations down a margin. The only rail reference is the board and the departure display, at full scale.

## Data

The same fictional `sample.json`. The LED board reads each trip's year, month, kind, places and `kanji_places`; the ticker line and the stat block compute 5 trips, 103 days, 6 groups and 162 travellers from the records.

## What it would take to become a `Template`

Components: `station-board` (three name scripts, line colour, previous and next labels), `departure-board` (rows from trips, kind-to-colour map, dot-matrix mask), `pictogram` (a keyed set), `meeting-point` (availability and offer), and `paving` (a strip). The line colour and badge would be theme values. A candidate needs a katakana rendering of their name as a data field (`name.kana`), checked by a speaker. More than five trips makes a longer board, or a limit of the most recent five with a ticker total.

## Open weaknesses

- The Safe tier leaves about 25 mm empty above the foot; a real CV would move content up or fill page 2.
- Creative repeats the facts compactly on the plates; the experience detail is thinner than in the other tiers.
- The LED dot mask darkens amber text slightly on photocopy. Legible, but the weakest element in black and white.
- A station clock around the portrait is a strong Japan-rail cue, but clocks are also generic. It is kept to the Creative tier only.
- The katakana of the name must be checked by a Japanese speaker for every client (here マルコス・イリアディス).
- Spacious Safe is the quietest of the nine Spacious pages: the domain rests on the board, the LED record, the pictograms and the paving strip.
- Spacious Stylish has no experience section on page 1; the current job is on the board's neighbour line (Lazuli Travel, τώρα · 03/2023–) and the jobs are on page 2.
- The new pictograms (headcount, vouchers, yen, coach, speaker) are original but plain; a `Template` would need one checked, consistent set.
- The Spacious pages were balanced by hand for this record. A `Template` needs a page-plan rule for one to three pages.
