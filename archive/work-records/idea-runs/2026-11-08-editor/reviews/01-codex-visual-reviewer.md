# Codex visual review round 1

Snapshot: Supplied “Rhumb Lines” renders; Stylish / Condensed, one-page concept; scan-first bar.
Batch sheets: `builds/codex-visual-review-20261108-173925/codex-sheet-rhumb-lines-colour.png` and `builds/codex-visual-review-20261108-173925/codex-sheet-rhumb-lines-mono.png`.

## Rhumb Lines, colour: FINDINGS

First impression: An antique navigation chart, with a commanding name and an intricately drawn compass portrait.

Scan Test: Pass. Name first, followed by role; both job titles, employers and dates are readily located. Education has a clear destination in the lower third, although its typography is considerably denser.

- **1 — Batch Test: Pass.** At row 1, column 4, the page is immediately identifiable. The coherent cartographic composition and strong hierarchy distinguish it from the surrounding templates.
- **3 — Flagship Parity: Fail.** The compass and typographic identity have comparable confidence. The reading surface lacks the Flagship’s disciplined separation between illustration and information.
- **4 — Distinct: Pass within the supplied evidence.** Compass, rhumb lines, scale-like rules and journey diagrams form one organising idea. A compass is familiar travel imagery, but the developed visual system avoids looking like a stock icon pasted into a template. Earlier concepts and Anti-examples were not supplied; comparison with them is unavailable.
- **5 — Reads as a CV: Pass.** Name, role, employment and credentials remain recognisable in the supplied A4 composition. The portrait appears cut out against the compass’s flat cream disc; no separate photographic scene is visible. The face sits slightly above the disc’s centre.
- **D1 Blocking (6 — Craft; also affects 3):** The rhumb-line network continues through the summary, job headings and dense experience text. Particularly around the first job, diagonal strokes cross letters while the strong vertical axis runs through the reading area. The illustration competes with information at the point where the scan-first brief requires clarity. **Fix:** Locally suppress or substantially lighten lines behind text, retaining their strength in open areas and around the compass. Preserve the chart concept without visible strokes through glyphs.

## Rhumb Lines, mono: FINDINGS

First impression: A striking black-and-white chart, with an especially assertive compass and name.

Scan Test: Pass. Name and job titles lead; dates occupy a consistent left rail, employers sit beside titles, and education is clearly labelled below.

- **1 — Batch Test: Pass.** At row 2, column 2, the compass silhouette and structured, full-page composition distinguish it even among coloured competitors.
- **3 — Flagship Parity: Fail.** The artwork and section rules show confidence, but the overlapping background geometry compromises the clean reading surface achieved by the Flagship.
- **4 — Distinct: Pass within the supplied evidence.** The cartographic idea survives independently of colour. Earlier concepts and Anti-examples are unavailable.
- **5 — Reads as a CV: Pass.** The information hierarchy survives monochrome. Behind the cut-out person is a uniform light-grey compass disc, with the face slightly high in its frame.
- **D2 Blocking (6 — Craft; also affects 3):** Solid, dashed and dotted background lines still traverse the body copy. The stronger grey diagonals are particularly intrusive across experience. **Fix:** Apply the same text-clearance treatment as the colour version.
- **Monochrome treatment: Pass otherwise.** Differentiated line patterns, white ground and decisive blacks make this a considered print design. The compass retains tonal separation; fills do not become muddy.

# Codex visual review round 1

Snapshot: Five styles, Stylish / Condensed / page 1; colour above, monochrome below. Thumbnail lineup review only.
Batch sheets: n/a — the supplied lineup is not a batch sheet.

## Lineup of five styles: PASS

First impression: Poster Sun attracts the eye first through its broad illustrated header; Timetable follows with the strongest typographic hierarchy and most decisive organisation.

**Distinct — PASS.** The five styles have clearly different organising ideas: navigational chart, postal envelope, transport timetable, illustrated travel poster and identity document. None could readily be mistaken for another at this scale.

Airmail and Guilloche are the closest relatives: both use travel-document conventions, security details and a contained portrait. Their compositions nevertheless separate them convincingly. Airmail makes the entire page an envelope; Guilloche places an identity card above a conventional information area. Rhumb Lines and Poster Sun both give the portrait a radial surround, but their overall page structures remain distinct.

These are familiar travel motifs, particularly the compass, airmail stripes and passport card. Familiarity alone does not make these interchangeable market templates: each motif visibly shapes its page. Guilloche has the greatest risk of feeling like a familiar passport-themed résumé because its distinctive treatment is concentrated in the header. Earlier concepts and Anti-examples are not attached, so resemblance to those cannot be assessed.

**Colour ranking: stopping power combined with professional, generic travel positioning**

1. **Poster Sun.** Strongest immediate presence. The landscape, sun and generous name treatment communicate travel without tying the candidate to Japan. Its main tension is that the illustration commands considerably more attention than the employment history.
2. **Timetable.** Strongest professional first impression. The large name, disciplined bands and tabular organisation make the information itself visually compelling. Its travel association is quieter but coherent.
3. **Airmail.** Memorable and unmistakably travel-related. The border gives it excellent recognition, although the small internal text competes with a relatively forceful perimeter.
4. **Rhumb Lines.** Elegant, distinctive silhouette and appealing cartographic craft. At this size, fine lines and delicate typography produce less immediate authority than the first three.
5. **Guilloche.** Recognisable travel-document character, but the pale header and dense lower information area give it the weakest presence among its siblings.

**Monochrome ranking: strength as an independent black-and-white design**

1. **Timetable.** Strong black anchors, clear white space and alternating bands preserve the hierarchy exceptionally well.
2. **Poster Sun.** The landscape becomes a confident black graphic; the radial portrait device remains legible and distinctive.
3. **Rhumb Lines.** The compass gains crispness, and the fine chart lines remain visually subordinate. The lighter body typography limits its impact.
4. **Airmail.** The envelope idea survives clearly, but the grey striped border retains considerable visual weight while losing the colour version’s useful differentiation.
5. **Guilloche.** The identity-card concept survives, but its pale patterning and concentric rings have the least tonal separation at thumbnail size.

- D1 Note (Distinct): Guilloche’s lower page contributes relatively little to its identity. Fix: carry one restrained security-rule or document-label treatment into the section structure.
- D2 Note (Batch Test impression): Airmail’s perimeter competes with its information. Fix: slightly reduce the border’s weight to strengthen the interior hierarchy.
