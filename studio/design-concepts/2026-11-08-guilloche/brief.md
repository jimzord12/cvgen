# Guilloche

**Idea:** the one-page CV opens under a band engraved like security print: a woven underprint on a rainbow tint, a large engraved travel globe with dotted routes and a drawn airliner, and the cut-out portrait in an arch on its own engraved ground, running down behind the band's twisted engraved foot. The engraved line carries on below as the section rules and as the rule between the columns. Three words: **engraved security print**.

Run: idea run 2026-11-08 (editor), five one-page styles for a practical travel CV under the `scan-first` bar. `Domain` travel; specialty group and private travel; `Rank` Tour Leader / Travel Consultant. One design, `Condensed` density, `stylish` `Design Tier`, drawn twice: in its own palette and as a monochrome version.

## Round 2: no longer a document

Both judges found that round 1 read as an imitation ID (design D9, Codex D1 to D4, research R6). Round 2 keeps the engraving and drops the document apparatus:

- **Card opened into a band.** The enclosing card is now a full-width header band. Its foot is an engraved border.
- **Document marks removed.** The machine-readable zone is gone, and with it the nationality code and the birth date in YYMMDD form. The ghost portrait and the "Surname, given name" and "Position" labels are gone too.
- **Name in reading order.** It is set as "Nikos Kontos".
- **Globe in the rosette.** The rosette's centre now holds an engraved globe of meridians and parallels.
- **Portrait on the band.** It stands in an arch drawn with one engraved line. The band's own tint and underprint show behind the cut-out; there is no backdrop rectangle (Codex D2/D4).
- **Mono engraved darker.** The engraving is black at 0.25 pt instead of grey hairlines, so the band holds at thumbnail size (design D14).
- **Engraving carried into the body.** A vertical engraved rule runs between the body's two columns, on top of the engraved section rules (Codex lineup D1).

## Round 3: a finished foot

Design D19 (Blocking) found about 43 mm of empty paper at the foot and a centre rule running on past both columns; Codex D1/D2 flagged the same rule tail.

- **Centre rule:** now measured against the two columns and ends with the longer one.
- **Deeper band:** the header band is 15 mm deeper (97 mm). The portrait arch grew from 52 × 62 to 60 × 76 mm, and the name went from 30 to 32 pt with more air around the fields.
- **Opened body:** body text 9.9 pt (was 9.6), section heads 7.6 mm apart (was 5.6), more space between bullets and between education rows.
- **Stronger rules:** the engraved section rules are a little taller and heavier (Codex lineup D1).
- **Closed foot:** the page now ends on the same engraved border as the band's foot. The repeated microprint line is cut (Codex: it read as a banknote flourish); the "FICTIONAL CANDIDATE · DESIGN CONCEPT" disclosure line stays, as on every concept.
- **Field labels:** left as they are (D20).

## Round 4: two Nits (D21 and a duplicate)

- **Rosette (D21):** the outer ring was about 0.4 mm from the right page edge. The rosette now sits about 4 mm inside it (centre moved from 184 to 181 mm, outer radius 22.5 mm), clear of printer clipping.
- **No duplicate availability:** the header grid no longer has an "Available from" field; the Availability section carries it. The specialty moved out of the grid to a line under the title ("Group and private travel, Europe and Asia"), so the grid is a balanced 3 × 2: phone, email, passport on the left; base, date of birth, nationality on the right.

## Round 5 (owner)

The owner's notes on this design (2026-11-08): "the image should have a different bg and it should placed behind that helix kind shape. Next the globe on the right, I think something more travel like would be better, like showing the globe bigger but with an airplane travel on it with dots lines showing its destination". Colour and mono both follow:

- **Portrait ground.** The arch now has its own ground: a mid-tone sage green from the palette (mid grey in mono), with a fine white engraved halo of two lobed rings behind the head. Reason: a mid tone is the one value that separates both the dark hair and the white shirt from the ground, and the engraved halo keeps it in the security-print idea rather than a studio backdrop. The image is centred with `fit: "cover"`, so the face stays centred and a little high.
- **Behind the helix.** The arch now runs down behind the band's foot. The twisted engraved band (now 6.8 mm, on a light veil) crosses the arch's lower edge in front of the shirt, well below the face.
- **Travel globe.** The rosette rings are gone and the globe is about two and a half times larger: 45 mm across, with a double rim, more meridians and parallels, and the equator heavier. Dotted routes curve from a home dot to four destination dots, two west and north in Europe and two east and south-east in Asia, with no labels and no country emphasis. An airliner flies the eastern route, drawn here as an engraved outline with a fuselage line and engine pods: no stock glyph (cf. Poster Sun, D11). Its outer rim stays about 4.6 mm inside the right page edge (D21), and the globe sits clear of the name, title and fields.

## What the owner should notice first

The band: a mint-to-peach-to-lilac tint and waves of fine woven lines across it. At right, the large engraved globe with its dotted routes and the airliner. At left, the portrait in an arch on its sage, engraved ground, passing behind the twisted engraved foot. Between them, the name in reading order with the title in rose.

## References and the principle taken from each

1. Guilloche and rainbow printing, EUIPO anti-counterfeiting technology guide, https://euipo.europa.eu/anti-counterfeiting-and-anti-piracy-technology-guide/marking-technologies/guillocherainbow-printing: repeating patterns of engraved lines, and offset colours that "blend gradually". **Principle:** the rainbow tint across the band and the woven underprint.
2. "Geometric and Floral Banknote Design", Stephen Gooden, 1954, Bank of England Museum, https://artsandculture.google.com/asset/geometric-and-floral-banknote-design/VgFBE3kAP4xNqQ. **Principle:** the rosette as a precise engraved ornament whose intricacy is the point. It is drawn here from equations (phase-shifted lobed circles), not copied.
3. Geometric lathe (Wikipedia), https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometric_lathe: the machine that cuts "intersecting and interlacing patterns of fine lines" for banknote plates. **Principle:** interlacing fine lines as the whole ornament. My own extension, not the article's: here they are generated by small Typst functions (lobes, amplitude, phase count) rather than traced.

The round-1 reference to the machine-readable passport (Wikipedia) is withdrawn: this design no longer uses a machine-readable zone.

## Why it differs

- **From the Flagship:** a light engraved band instead of a dark one, and security engraving and a globe instead of engine-room tools.
- **From the other four:** the only engraved, generated line ornament; the only humanist sans plus monospace voice; the only arch portrait.
- **From earlier concepts:** Stamp Rally was "the escort's travel papers": a boarding pass, rubber stamps and luggage tags. This uses no paper, ticket, stamp or document form, only the engraving craft. Design D13 noted the card head was close to Stamp Rally's boarding-pass head; the open band removes that card structure. It is not near either `Anti-example`.
- **From the market:** in my own observation (not a sourced survey), online builders do not use engraving; the nearest they come is a hairline.

## What it would take to become a `Template`

- **Artwork pack:** `rosette(r, amp, lobes, phases)`, `waveband(width, height, lines, periods)`, `vwaveband`, `globe(r)`, `routes(home, stops, plane-on)`, `airliner(size)`, `arch-portrait(w, h, ground)`; all parametric. The routes' destinations would come from data (offsets on the globe), never labelled. The centre rule needs the columns measured, as here.
- **Theme:** ink, two engraving colours, the band tint (or white for mono), Fira Sans and Plex Mono.
- **Data:** all fields exist in the travel contract.
- **Performance:** the rosettes and bands are thousands of curve points. The compile is still quick, and the colour PDF is about 0.68 MB with the portrait.

## Known weaknesses

- **Small field labels:** the band still uses small monospace labels above the contact fields; they are lighter than before but keep a faint form-like note.
- **Lower page:** below the band the page is calm; its identity there rests on the engraved rules alone.
- **Text breaks:** running text binds its last three or four words together (a non-breaking tie), and a school name breaks at its comma.
